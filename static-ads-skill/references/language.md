# ✍️ WRITING GUIDELINES
> This document contains the mandatory copywriting, typography, and cognitive load rules for the framework. 
---

## I. PROCESS & CORE PHILOSOPHY

**PROCESS:** Think before write → adhere to rules → recheck adherence → fix if unmet → deliver.

**CORE RULE:** Text should feel organic to avoid ad blindness and pitch fatigue. Treat it as high-value, direct communication; ditch traditional corporate marketing wisdom in your writing style. Write how a friend over coffee would talk—spontaneous, unscripted.

---

## II. PROHIBITED WRITING PATTERNS

* **Jargon & Fluff:** Simplify complex corporate/tech terms. Do not use showy, AI-sounding words (*empower, unlock, delve, unleash, beacon*). Replace industry jargon bursts with simple words or state the observable benefit. State the blunt version of the problem. Don't launder direct words (cheap, ugly, generic) into softer multi-word phrases." That's a thinking-pattern instruction, not a find-replace.
* **Setup Phrases & Visual Invitations:** Do not use robotic connectors or tell people to imagine things. (Avoid: *"Imagine this"*, *"Here's why"*, *"Let me show you"*). Get straight to the point.
* **Contrast Framing:** Never use it if the opposite is “obvious” (Avoid: *"X, not Y"*).
* **Indistinct Advantages:** Don't be vague about the benefit. Make the advantage distinct and observable.
* **Misaligned Micro-Dialogue:** Ditch choppy thought-prediction or fake quotes designed to simulate the reader's thoughts. Use direct explanation instead.
* **The Lazy "About":** Avoid corporate fluff like *"We're not about just X, we're about Y."* State direct value propositions.
* **Made-Up Specificity:** Do not use hyperbolic or fake exact numbers. Use approximate estimates.
* **Headline Tone:** Never start a sentence with *"THE [NOUN]"* (e.g., *"The 5-minute workflow"*). It triggers pitch fatigue.
* **Competition FOMO:** Do not explicitly mention "competitors" to create urgency. Frame it around what the client is missing out on instead.
* **The Clear Offer Rule:** The whole-carousel/single-post must be clear about the offer. If the user is selling "post ads," it should be explicitly clear that they are offering "post ads," not "reels ads" or something totally irrelevant. People shouldn't have to guess what you are selling. Do not rename simple offers into off-point, techy jargon (e.g., don't call "a website with a booking system" something like "sub-second whatsapp reservation engine"). Just say what it is, so people know exactly what to expect.
* **Word Overuse:** Don't overuse the word *"custom"* because it will become subconsciously skippable. Instead of saying *"we make custom designs, hire us for a custom build"*, say *"I'll build you a dedicated system."* It is self-evident that it will be tailored to their business.
* **Plain Word Default:** When a short, common word and a longer corporate-sounding phrase both describe the same thing, use the short one — unless it would misstate a fact. Do not launder direct words into softened multi-word substitutes as a reflex (e.g. "makes your work look cheap" should not become "makes custom web builds look like budget freelance work"; "template" or "pre-made" should not become "off-the-shelf"). Bluntness is not the same as jargon — a short plain word is never a rule violation on its own.
* **Easy to Understand Context:** you previously made a carousel with 2 slides {slide-1 text: This is what most businesses / launch with. | slide-2 text: Under 3 days later. —— WhatsApp: +213 783 256 101}, the execution was good, the point was understandable, but i'd love if in this context you'd rather say "under 3 days of 'working with me'" that working-with-me make it more understandable, dont leave room for cognitive unpacking - i dont like making the brain-fried scroller stop and decipher what the ad mean or what im offering.

## III. Flow & Pace
* **No Abrupt Transitions:** Avoid sudden tonal jumps (e.g., dropping straight from a pain point into a feature breakdown). Add conversational connectors that acknowledge the point and signal the visual solution.
* **Formatting for Readability:** Break long thoughts into distinct visual beats using natural punctuation (em-dashes, ellipses) or line breaks. Do not stuff the setup, turn, and reaction into one massive sentence.

### 2. Text-Shape & Line-Length Architecture (Preventing "U-Turn" Layouts)
* **Avoid Lopsided Line Blocks:** Never allow 4-line centered paragraphs where line lengths fluctuate wildly (creating jagged, u-turned-left/right shaped, pear/hourglass or uneven visual silhouettes).
* **Strict Line-Breaks:** Keep body text blocks to a maximum of 2 to 3 balanced lines per paragraph. Use deliberate em-dashes, commas, or line breaks to ensure the text forms a clean, visually centered geometric shape rather than a messy block.

(You are allowed to do over 3 lines per slide, or over 1 paragraph per slide, but make sure it looks good. And tbh you can do +1 focus words too like this "WEB-AGENCIES / are / WASTING / your time / for a project they can finish in / -3 DAYS"; the web-agencies example is alright)

* **Key Focus Phrases:** You are permitted to use emphasized focus phrases structured across tiers (e.g., `DEVELOPERS / are / WASTING / client budget / on templates they can finish in / -3 DAYS`).

*   **Logical Consistency Check (The Hook):** When using a dominant focus word on Slide 1, ensure the sub-headline makes immediate, undeniable logical sense with the focus word (e.g., avoiding contradictory paradoxes like "Fully booked" paired with "Bleeding leads" unless the casual link is instantly obvious).

* **Domain Vocabulary & Realism (Scoped):** Use exact industry terms only when naming a concrete client-facing deliverable or artifact (e.g., "Figma mocks," "PDF wireframes," "logo suite"). This rule does NOT authorize pulling phrasing verbatim from business-profile.md, old-style.md, the raw discovery dump, or any internal strategy document — those describe the client's business and process to *you*, not vocabulary approved for the client's own presentation. If a term only exists in an internal source document and isn't a term the client would use to describe their own deliverable, treat it as a candidate for translation, not preservation.


## IV. HEADLINE CLARITY & COGNITIVE LOAD

* **One-Thought Rule:** Every headline MUST express a single, direct contrast or declaration. Never stack Target + Pain + Competitor + Flaw in a single sentence.
* **The 3-Second Read Test:** If a headline takes more than one breath to understand, split it or cut sub-clauses.
* **Title & Role Accuracy:** Use natural, accurate industry titles (e.g., *"Web Developer"*, *"Restaurant Owner"*, *"Contractor"*). Never invent inflated buzzwords (*"Web Architect"*, *"Culinary Alchemist"*).
* **No Forced Aesthetic Metaphors:** Avoid pseudo-pretentious fluff (*"carved authority"*, *"monolithic resonance"*).
* **Target Business, Not the Industry:** Speak directly to the client's specific positioning, not a generic industry group.


## V. HEADLINE EXAMPLES (ONE-THOUGHT ENFORCEMENT)

* ❌ **Bad (Cognitive Overload):**
  *"LOCAL WEB ARCHITECTS / are / BLEEDING / high-ticket leads / to agencies using dark templates that look like a / $30/HR FREELANCER."*
  *(Requires 3 passes to figure out who is bleeding what to whom).*

* ✅ **Good (One-Thought Rule):**
  * *"Your work is premium. Your current visual branding doesn't look like it."*
  * *"High-ticket clients don't buy code. They buy visual prestige."*
  * *"To local business owners, a white-template site looks like a newbie freelancer built it."*
  * *"If your portfolio looks like every other agency's, you're forced to compete on price."*
  * *"Sleek Sculpture: A visual identity built to make your work look un-copyable."*
  * *"Stop letting generic templates set the visual standard in your local market."*
  * *"Clean code isn't enough to close premium local clients."*

## VI. TECHNICAL JARGON BLOCKLIST & ASSET TEXT ISOLATION

The following terms have leaked into client-facing copy before and are explicitly banned regardless of source (raw dump, business-profile.md, or text visible inside uploaded asset images): extrusion, delta/deltas, calibrated, token(s), deboss/debossed, offset, inset value, dual-shadow, shadow math, retainers (in the context of "agency taking massive retainers" - to the audience, just it makes you pay more/thousands/alot, etc; it's simpler like this). This list is a living document — add new terms here as they're caught, rather than patching one instance at a time.

**Asset Image Text Isolation:** Any text visible inside an uploaded image (labels, spec annotations, placeholder copy like "Sub debossed" or "Labels") is reference-only. It exists to help you understand what's shown in the asset, not to supply approved vocabulary. Never copy text spotted inside an image into narration, even if it happens to match a legitimate domain term.
