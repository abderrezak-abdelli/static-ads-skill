**1. The Dynamic Narrative Arc**

* Do not default to a fixed number of slides. Adapt the length and structure to the specific offer, the audience, and the psychological journey required.
* **The Hook (Slide 1):** This is the most critical slide. It must immediately grab the target audience using whatever psychological angle works best for them (e.g., validating their high status, calling out a specific pain, etc.). It should look like a native, organic piece of content.
* **The CTA (Final Slide):** Never use a dry, valueless command (e.g., "DM me"). The CTA must clearly propose a high-value, low-friction reason to take action (e.g., "DM the word 'DEMO' and I will make your free demo").

> The CTA is very important, make sure you get explicit direction from the user (be careful business-profile may mention a cta for a different purpose dont assume its for the meta ads or what's business used to unless obvious), by default consider for meta ads cta is either click > landing page or click > dm

**2. The Environmental Anchor**

* Create a seamless swipe experience by using a consistent base environment across all slides (e.g., a specific type of desk, a continuous background surface, or a distinct spatial setting).
* **Crucial:** This anchor must *not* prevent the mood from changing. The base environment stays the same, but the lighting, state of objects, and atmosphere must shift dynamically.

**3. The Visual Mood Journey**

* Use lighting, color temperature, and shadow to subliminally influence the reader's emotions and match the text's subtext.
* *Example Application:* Use warm, crisp spotlights to build trust/premium feel; shift to harsh lighting or heavy, consuming shadows to emphasize a pain point or disservice; return to bright, clean lighting to introduce the solution/hope.

**4. Text-to-Visual Balance**

* There is no strict word cap, but you must avoid design blunders. Write enough words to convey the meaning perfectly and build an understandable, connected story, but keep it concise enough that the text doesn't overcrowd the visual.
* Make it easy to chew and digest.

**5. Standalone Prompting (The "Fresh Session" Rule)**

* Because the image generator will have no memory of the overarching concept, *every single prompt* must explicitly state:
* The environmental anchor (e.g., "deep black obsidian desk").
* The exact lighting and mood (e.g., "cinematic dark mode, harsh cool shadows").
* The conceptual color palette (Never use hex codes or literal color names; use descriptive guidelines like "electric-green interactive elements" or "deep black surfaces").
* The exact typography style (e.g., "clean, elegant, thin serif luxury typography in crisp white").
* The precise on-screen text to be rendered.

### 1. Word-Break & Line-Formatting Rules
* **No Hyphenated Word Splits:** Never split a single word across lines with a hyphen (e.g., `Des- / -ign`). Keep words intact.
* **Numeric Formatting:** Use numbers (`3 days`, `$10k`, `72 hours`) instead of spelling them out (`three days`, `ten thousand dollars`) to keep text compact, scannable, and punchy.

### 2. Conversational Rhythm & Focus Word Placement
* **Avoid Unnatural First-Word Forced Hooks:**
  * ❌ *Bad:* `COMMODITY? / Stop building for other developers.`
* **Natural Focal Placement:** Focus words can sit anywhere in the sentence as long as they carry natural cadence:
  * ✅ *Good:* `WEB-AGENCIES / are / WASTING / your time / for a project they can finish in / -3 DAYS.`

### **TARGET AUDIENCE RULE:** 

FRAME FOR THE BUYER’S REALITY, NOT THE INSIDER’S PROCESS

When creating content to generate leads or educate prospects, always write from the perspective of the buyer’s business outcome, never the seller’s internal execution. Insiders analyze how a service/product gets built; buyers only care about what it solves, costs, or saves.

**The Golden Rule:** If a hook or headline sounds like something two industry peers would discuss, debate, or complain about among themselves, it is wrong. It will only attract competitors and practitioners. Rewrite it to address the buyer's risk, preparation, or bottom line.

**Core Guidelines for Content Framing:**

**Shift from Meta-Process to Actionable Preparation:**

❌ Insider/Peer Hook: "Why [Service/Product Delivery] takes 6 weeks instead of 6 days." (Attracts peers analyzing process)

✅ Buyer-Centric Hook: "Prepare these 3 things before hiring [Provider] so your project doesn't get delayed by months." (Attracts ready-to-buy clients)

**Shift from Technical Features to Business Impact:**

❌ Insider/Peer Hook: "Understanding the difference between [Technical Method A] and [Technical Method B]." (Attracts students/enthusiasts)

✅ Buyer-Centric Hook: "How choosing the wrong [Method/Product Type] bleeds your monthly profit." (Attracts decision-makers)

**Shift from Pricing Justification to Risk Avoidance:**

❌ Insider/Peer Hook: "Why high-end [Product/Service] costs so much to produce." (Attracts creators/vendors)

✅ Buyer-Centric Hook: "How to spot overhyped [Services/Products] and avoid paying for features you don't need." (Attracts budget owners)

**Pre-Output Check:** Before finalizing any piece of content, ask: "Would a business owner/buyer search/watch this to solve a problem today, or is this just an insider explaining how the engine works?" Focus entirely on the buyer's problem.
