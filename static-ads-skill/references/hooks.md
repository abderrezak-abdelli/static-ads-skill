### **III. HOOK ARCHITECTURE & STRATEGY**

## What makes a HOOK work

A Hook has two roles: grab attention + trigger curiosity.

To nail both you need to understand them on a subtext level.

1. Attention = Pattern Interrupt
People scroll like zombies.
Your job is to:
- Slap the subconscious.
- Break the brain’s autopilot.
- Surprise the viewer.

Use pattern interrupts.

2. Curiosity = Trigger Skepticism
• Make the viewer *doubt* your point. Trigger a BS reaction.
• Because humans are naturally drawn to beliefs that oppose them, and have a deep need to correct others.
• Once you trigger skepticism you won their attention, they will stick to listen/judge/correct your view

This doesn’t mean you should lie, no, you should reframe your statements in a non conventional manner.


A powerful hook doesn’t just say something—it *disrupts the noise*.

Examples:
- “This is Why NO ONE watches your first 3 seconds.”
(Hits insecurity + starts with blame)
- “You’re not failing because of your content. It’s your HOOK.”
(Challenges assumption, opens a loop)

## Common HOOK Mistakes

Starting with greetings or intros
“Hey guys, welcome back…” = instant scroll. You’re warming them up while they’re cooling off.

Trying to sound smart instead of being relevant
The hook isn’t your TED Talk—it’s your street pitch. Get real or get skipped.

Over-explaining or giving the punchline too early
If they already “get it” in 3 seconds, they won’t need the next minute.

Using vague curiosity without proof it’s real
Hooks like “this changed my life” or “watch what happens next” only work if the viewer already trusts you.
Otherwise? It’s empty suspense.


Instead: replace “this” with the specific threat, benefit, or belief you’re challenging.

Get specific to spice up curiosity, specificity makes claims more believable.

## The 5 HOOKS You Need to Use
Each one is built to *interrupt the scroll* and *trigger curiosity*.

1 Shock Value Hook
“99% of people do [X] wrong.”
• Example: “99% of creators ruin their Reel in the first 3 seconds.”
• This one feeds ego and FOMO—nobody wants to be in the 99%.
• When to Use: When calling out a common mistake or myth.

2 Contrarian Hook
“Why [Popular Belief] is a Myth.”
• Example: “This is why saying ‘hook your audience’ is bad advice.”
• This triggers a B.S. reaction—people watch just to argue (or agree).
• When to Use: When your angle opposes mainstream tips.

3 Pain Point Hook
“Tired of [struggling with X]?”
• Example: “Tired of posting Reels no one watches?”
• This makes them feel seen and subtly blames them (in a good way).
• When to Use: When targeting exhausted, discouraged people.

4 Myth/Misconception Hook
• Subtext-Example: “You’ve been doing X wrong all along.”

5 The "Lazy" Promise Hook
This is for the 95% of people who want results without the work.
They scroll past “10x your traffic” because it sounds exhausting.
But they stop for the path of least resistance.

Examples:
- “How to look expensive on a budget”
- “Get paid to scroll TikTok”
- “The laziest way to learn Python”
